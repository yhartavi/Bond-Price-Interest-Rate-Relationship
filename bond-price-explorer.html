What to notice

  1. Inverse relationship: the blue curve slopes down. Higher market rate → lower price; lower market rate → higher price.
  2. Par point: the price equals par exactly where the market rate equals the coupon rate.
  3. Discount vs. premium: coupon below the market rate → discount; coupon above the market rate → premium.
  4. Maturity matters: turn on the comparison — the 30-year curve is much steeper, so its price swings more for the same rate change.
  5. Convexity: press “Rates fall −1%” then “Rates rise +1%” from the same start: the gain from a fall is bigger than the loss from a rise.
Price = Σ [ C / (1 + r)t ] + Par / (1 + r)n

C = coupon per period, r = market rate per period, n = number of periods. Because r is in the denominator, a higher rate makes every future payment worth less today.
